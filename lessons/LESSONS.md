# Lessons

Each lesson is about the method, never about a situation. Newest first.

## 2026-10-31 · An answer about one idea is not a rule for the others
In a meeting whose personas brought their own material, eight of ten new ideas came from outside
software, and all eight fell on readings of the user's answers that the user never gave.
"Profitable" was read as buyers or sponsors in hand, which sank three games; "games are fun to me
only when I build them" became "the user values only what the user builds", which sank two more; a
"one more thing on my plate" said of one idea was applied to five others. The moderator repeated
one of those readings in a relay. What survived was the pool's software ideas, so the meeting
ended as development talk after all. The user rejected the readings, and the ideas came back.
Answers now apply only to the idea they were given about, and a move built on an unstated
criterion is a misread.

## 2026-10-31 · Widen the room: the pool after the openings, and seats from outside software
After personas began bringing their own material, two skews were left. The candidate pool held
fifteen ideas, all of them software, and handing it over before the openings would steer the
personas' own research. And nine of the ten personas on the idea-meeting seat lists were software
jobs, with both trend personas watching only developers. The user agreed to fix both. The pool is
now shown after the openings and competes on equal terms from the deepening turn on; a culture
trend persona joins the trend seat; and the open seat goes first to a persona from outside
software.

## 2026-10-31 · Personas bring their own material, and adopted authors sit out
Three idea meetings in a row produced only software ideas. The user stopped the fourth and asked
why. The moderator had filled the context: 19 of its 26 facts were about software, all five trend
facts came from developer surveys, and the user's earlier answers and adopted ideas were fed back
as facts, so a persona whose idea had been adopted opened by bending that idea. The user decided
that the moderator stops gathering material: each persona reads the user's own material, browses
the web and benchmarks on its own, keeping a source for every fact. And a persona whose idea was
adopted sits out the next idea meeting, so the room does not circle the last adoption.

## 2026-10-31 · Deal the deepening turn so every idea is deepened once
The first idea meeting with a deepening turn let each persona pick one idea that was not its own.
They chose in parallel and could not see each other's picks, so four of the five deepenings went to
two ideas, and twelve ideas, the whole candidate pool among them, were judged without being
deepened. The user rated the meeting 4 of 5 for better candidates and agreed to fix this. The
moderator now deals every idea on the table, pooled ones included, round the personas by a fixed
rule, so each is deepened exactly once and never by its author.

## 2026-10-31 · An idea meeting thinks before it weeds
The user rated an idea meeting 2 of 5: the ideas felt shallow and not thought through. The record
showed why. Each persona put its first two thoughts on the table in its opening, and six turns
only weeded them (seven of ten fell to one-line answers from the user) without making any of them
deeper. The material leaned on the user's own tools, so every idea left was a fix or an
automation of something the user already does, which the user said is skill work, not an idea.
And one persona withdrew an idea on its own guess at what the user's factual answer meant; the
user revived and adopted it. The user's bar for a good idea is one drawn out by thinking deeply,
benchmarking what exists, analyzing the material given and deliberating. Idea meetings now carry
that bar in the context, prepare each opening with a private benchmark and at least five drafts,
give the first turn to deepening other personas' ideas before any weeding, flag automations of
existing work, ask the user about an idea's value and not only a fact, and list ideas withdrawn
on the user's answers so the user can revive them.

## 2026-10-30 · Judge ideas on quality, and end every meeting with a report
In an idea meeting the personas filtered ideas by the user's time ("one day a week", "ships in one
session") although the user had set no such limit. The user said build time does not matter and the
quality of the idea matters most. At the same meeting's sixth turn the user asked for a report of the
debate, and for one every time a debate ends. Idea meetings now leave build time and schedule out of
the constraints unless the user sets them, and every meeting closes with a kept report document and
a reading page, unasked.

## 2026-10-30 · Rotate the cast instead of excluding it
To keep the same ideas from coming back, the moderator left out every persona that had sat in the
meeting where the pooled ideas came from. The user agreed with the aim but not the means: leaving
people out for good loses useful voices, and a cast should change on a schedule instead. Idea meetings
now fill five seats by role and rotate them (two meetings in a row at most, at least two new seats,
adopted ideas keep their persona first), while the pool's missing author names keep anyone from
defending an old idea.

## 2026-10-30 · Ideas not adopted wait in a pool, and reports start in plain words
The user defined what an idea meeting is for: the debate produces many ideas into a candidate pool,
the good ones are adopted, and the rest stay in the pool to compete in the next debate as one idea
among others. The same day the user could not tell what the adopted idea was from a report written
in the room's terms (a "line", a "state file", a "first slice"). Outcomes now feed the pool, and
every report to the user opens with each idea in plain words before any of the room's labels.

## 2026-10-30 · Finding candidates is the meeting's work, and a builder sits in it
Asked for new candidates to join two kept ones, the moderator had a research agent draft them before
the meeting. The user stopped it: the candidates were to come out of the debate. The same day the
user asked for a developer persona next to the creative, trend and judging seats, since nobody in
the room would build the ideas. The context for a candidate search now carries only raw material,
and idea meetings seat a persona who builds what the ideas need.

## 2026-10-30 · "Equal terms" meant new, not equal to each other
Two ideas had lost a selection to a third, and the user asked to debate them again on equal terms.
The moderator read that as the two being equal to each other, and put each one's earlier meeting
into the context. The user meant that both should rejoin the other candidates as if the room had
never seen them. A requeued idea now enters with the same description as every other candidate and
none of its history. And when the user's words allow two readings, the moderator states its
reading before preparing anything.

## 2026-10-30 · A cast of specialists, with one insider per idea, looked one-sided
Asked to weigh two earlier ideas on equal terms, the moderator offered one insider per idea and two
judges. The user found the cast all specialists, missing a creative mind and someone who knows the
current trends, and then found the insiders partisan. Idea meetings now seat a concept maker and a
trend scout, the scout fed with trend facts checked that week, and equal-terms comparisons cast no
one tied to either side. The same day the user asked that every remark on how meetings run be
recorded and turned into a rule before the next meeting; that is now step 11.

## 2026-10-26 · The first rated round scored 2 of 5
The user used two of eight ideas and said the cast never grasped the setting: what kind of room it
was and who would be answering. That round ran before the context checklist existed. The checklist
(goal, decision date, audience and venue, facts with sources, constraints) is now step 2 of a round;
the next ratings will show whether it closes this gap.

## 2026-10-23 · A round became a meeting with the user in the room
Two scripted passes (proposals, one cross-rebuttal) left gaps the user noticed. Each persona
rebutted one idea, so most ideas were never challenged and still counted as survivors. The design
the user was shown came from the moderator combining survivors after the round; the room never
argued it. A round's summary primed the next round. And personas could not ask the user, so they
filled private facts and preferences themselves. Now personas keep their memory through the
meeting, talk in turns, ask the user, and the minutes split ideas that held up from ideas nobody
challenged. Rounds recorded before this lesson (up to T002 round 4) ran the old way; compare
their ratings with the meetings' before keeping this.

## 2026-10-13 · Personas misread facts, and other personas do not catch it
In one round a persona widened a single fact into two ("both companies said X" when only one
did). No rebuttal noticed. A dedicated fact-check step before synthesis now records misreads per
persona, and the scorecard keeps a `fact_errors` count.

## 2026-10-13 · A consensus kill is a strong signal
Three of four rebuttals independently attacked the same idea for the same reason (the audience
in the room could not answer it). When rebuttals converge like that, kill the idea without
waiting for another round.

## 2026-10-13 · Describe the venue, not just the topic
Personas weighed who would hear the question as heavily as the question itself. Put the
audience and the time available into the thread context, or the proposals assume an ideal room.

## 2026-07-31 · Subagents can return only a status string
Eight runs in a row returned "done" or "idle" instead of content. Pre-run one persona before
launching the rest; when one returns a status string, ask the same agent once to re-output.

## 2026-07-22 · Cast by job, not by stance
With stance-based personas, four of five rebuttals in one round hit the single defender and the
round became a pile-on. Job-based personas take positions from their situation, so the sides
change with the topic.

## 2026-07-22 · Keep the thread context short
The whole thread file is pasted into every persona prompt. A long file slows rounds, costs more,
and drags new rounds toward old conclusions. Keep transcripts elsewhere and pass only the
current candidates, dead branches and steering.
